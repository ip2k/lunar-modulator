**Why.** The owner, 2026-10-06: "see what we can learn from
https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-x0x since that just launched. If
possible I'd love to get their 808 and 909 and 303 sounds into our firmware
as engines. What happened to all the other engines from Felucca too?"
Earlier permission (2026-10-05): "anything that could get us more drum
engines / sound engines / especially 808-style", with GPL code "gated with
a switch but ON by default, everywhere while we test".

## 0. Short answer

- **X0X is Felucca's platform with a new instrument.** It keeps Felucca's
  HAL, USB, update loader, storage and installer. It adds:
  - a kit after the TR-909 (9W9's circuit models, ER-99's sampled cymbals);
  - a kit after the TR-808 (8W8's circuit models);
  - two basses after the TB-303 (Open303 with the Devilfish ranges and a
    RAT drive), each with a TB-3PO acid generator;
  - a breakbeat generator (mestela's BB Gen, by permission);
  - song mode, knob motion, undo and autosave.

  It is all float DSP. It runs on FM-1s and reaches about 102 % of the CPU
  in its worst case [reported: README.md:21-22].
- **The ports are cheap in RAM and CPU, and checked against their
  originals.** Each part's tests pass against its upstream here (§2.6). On
  this Mac, with every voice struck on every 16th:

  | Part | RAM (i386) | Cost of a 64-frame block |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 909 kit | 7,232 B | 6.3 µs |
  | 808 kit | 3,352 B | 15.0 µs |
  | 303 | 752 B | 2.9 µs (4.0 µs with RAT drive) |
  | Our Drums, same pattern | 7,616 B (64-bit) | 19.3 µs |

  The 909's cymbal samples are the one large cost: 221 KB of flash as
  int16 (§2.1).
- **Port now, behind `FM1_GPL_MODS`** (§6): the 303, then TB-3PO as a MIDI
  effect, then both kits as pad kits. The break generator is **not**
  ported; §5 drafts a request to its author.
- **"What happened to Felucca's other engines?"** We took only FM6. We
  rebuilt it from msfa (Apache-2.0) and kept Felucca's `fm6_core.c` as a
  test oracle, because the rest is GPL-3.0-only and the GPL switch did not
  exist until 2026-10-05. They are integer engines bound to Felucca's
  voice, ADSR and modulation framework, so each needs a shim (§3).
- **Felucca ports, ranked** (§3.2), by how new a sound each adds:
  1. WHEEL, a tonewheel-style organ;
  2. TRIO, three oscillators with ring and sync, after 8-bit home-computer
     sound chips;
  3. SLICE, a slicer, with the ADPCM zone player from SAMPLE;
  4. PHASE, phase distortion after CrispyZebra.
- **Hardware facts that matter to us** (§1):
  - X0X measured the clock at **360 MHz**, not the 240 MHz in CLAUDE.md;
  - its I2S runs at about **44,145 Hz**;
  - **a float divide by zero traps on the AC79** when the EMU's div0 trap
    is on (X0X's first crash on hardware);
  - a flash erase silences audio.

### 1.2 What it teaches the state-file and song work

This compares X0X with `notes/2026-10-06-state-files.md` and
`notes/2026-10-06-song-and-scenes.md`, as they stand on
`docs/2026-10-06@state-song-design`.

**Storage** (X0X `firmware/src/storage.c`, `app/project.c`) [verified: source]:
- Each object has an A/B pair of 4 KB sectors. A save:
  1. erases the other copy;
  2. programs the payload at +256;
  3. programs the 32-byte header last, as the commit record;
  4. reads both back before it reports SAVED.
- On load, the valid copy with the higher sequence number wins. Payload
  and header each carry a CRC32.
- A project is six objects, each at most 3,840 bytes: settings, sound,
  patterns 1–8 and 9–16, the song, and the motion lanes.
- An FNV-1a hash of each object's last saved payload means a save writes
  only the objects that changed: usually one or two sectors.
- Each object starts with the format word. An object of another format
  loads as its defaults rather than being guessed at.
- Settings grow at the end: a shorter payload loads into the front of the
  struct, and the new fields keep 0, which is their default. New fields
  are chosen so that 0 is the old behaviour (brightness 0 = full).

Against our journal (state-files §14), which is more code but levels wear,
the parts worth taking [inferred]:
1. read back and check every record before the screen says it was saved;
2. skip records whose hash is unchanged;
3. append-only settings in which zero means "as before".

**Autosave.** X0X saves while stopped:
- 4 s after the last touch;
- at most every 20 s;
- only the objects that changed

[reported: `ui.c:3115-3133`]. Ours waits for SAVE, or 30 s idle at most
every 5 minutes, while stopped. Both agree that the transport must be
stopped.

**Undo by difference** (X0X `app/undo.c`, `ui.c:848-870`) [verified: source]:
- The region is the whole project after its settings. A shadow copy holds
  it as it was at the last commit.
- A commit stores the runs of changed bytes and their old contents. Undo
  swaps them in, and the same entry then serves redo.
- The pool is 24 KiB, holding at most 32 steps; the oldest steps drop
  first.
- A step is what changed between two quiet moments (400 ms with nothing
  touched), or one recording pass.
- So every edit can be undone without any code at the place that makes
  it.

For our device, the same could run over the binary record image (§8 of
the state note): about 16 KB of shadow plus a pool [inferred]. That is an
owner decision on RAM (§7, X10).

**The song** [verified: `seq/sequencer.h:43-60`]:
- One entry per bar, 192 bars: each part's pattern and its mute bits,
  1,156 bytes in all.
- PLAY starts at the selected bar. The song loops.
- SONG + REC writes live cues and mutes into the song from the start bar
  on, extending it past its end.

Our song is Movy's list of scene presses with repeat counts (64 entries).
The song note adds a Song page with "play from any entry" and end modes.
Two ideas worth keeping:
- **Record a performance into the list.** Movy's Loop-hold gesture
  already appends scenes. X0X also writes mutes, which a scene captures
  in Movy by being a snapshot of what plays.
- **A changed part restarts while the others run on.** X0X's per-part
  patterns behave as Movy's per-track clips launched at the bar. The two
  designs agree.

**Knob motion** [verified: `seq/motion.h`, `seq/motion.c`]:
- 160 lanes of 40 bytes in a pool. A lane is one knob's value on each of
  32 steps of one part's pattern.
- Recording writes only the steps the knob moved on. A gap of up to 4
  steps between two written steps is filled in a straight line.
- Playback ramps between set steps once per block, so sweeps are smooth.
- A knob turned while not recording wins for one pass.
- SAVE plus a turn clears that knob's lane.
- Playback writes the engine, never the saved sound.

Our Motion recordings belong to the mod rack (owner, 2026-10-06); the
ramp, the gap fill, "touch wins for a pass" and "never write the saved
value" carry over.

**User samples** [verified: `app/slots.c`; Felucca `eng_sample.c`]:
- Three slots of 80 KiB at flash `0xA0000`.
- Each slot has a 512-byte header (magic `FSMP`, up to 16 zones, CRC32)
  and IMA ADPCM at 22,050 Hz.
- Samples are read in place through the plain XIP window, with no copy in
  RAM.
- They are uploaded over SysEx (Felucca's editor protocol), with retries.

For a sample engine of ours (Chop, §3), a sample would be a record kind in
the gated store. Reading in place needs that region to be readable
unencrypted (docs/11, "Flash writes") [inferred].

**Identity.** Two forks already share `FM-1_909`, so an identity alone
cannot tell firmwares apart. The state note's SysEx tool insists on our
own `HELLO` and `INFO` reply as well as `FM-1_5xx`, and this supports
that rule.

## 2. X0X's sound parts, one by one

Common to all of them [verified: source]:
- C99, float only, no libm (X0X's `fastmath.h`), no `double`, no
  allocation.
- Built with `-Wdouble-promotion -Werror`.
- A fixed 44,100 Hz (`D9_SR`, `SR`, `BASS303_SR`, `BRK_FS`).
- At most 256 frames a call.
- Mono out. The kits also fill a reverb send and a delay send.
- Every user parameter is a 0–127 "pot" (`x0x_param.h`).

### 2.1 The kit after the TR-909 (`dsp/drum909*`)

- **What.** Eleven voices: BD, SD, LT, MT, HT, RS, CP, CH, OH, CR, RD.
  9W9's circuit models, ported line for line [reported: `drum909.c:2-6`;
  9W9 README]:
  - **kick:** a VCO near 49 Hz with the 909's additive-exponential sweep, a
    16 ms pitch hold, diode rounding and a beater click; optional Pitch and
    P.Dpth;
  - **snare:** two shells a 1.585 ratio apart, plus the ENV4 noise channel;
  - **toms:** three VCOs at 1 : 1.50 : 2.75, the upper two attack-only;
  - **rim:** a pulse shock-exciting two resonators;
  - **clap:** four bursts 12 ms apart into a noise band, and a room tail;
  - **hats, ride and crash:** ER-99's samples, int16 in flash, read at a
    32.32 position. Closed and open hat share one buffer and choke each
    other.
- **Per voice:** Drive, a choice of 7 distortion types (Diode, Clip, SAT,
  BFZ, PDIST, Fold, Crush) and two sends. **Kit:** Accent (1–4) and
  velocity depth. Voices have 5–8 pots each [verified: `drum909.c:46-104`].
- **Arithmetic** [reported: `drum909_dsp.h:6-23`]:
  - 9W9's Web Audio envelopes run as a recursion, re-anchored to the
    closed form at **every render call**;
  - phases are uint32, sample positions 32.32;
  - tanh is a 1,025-point table;
  - an idle voice costs nothing.
- **Numbers:**

  | | Value |
  | --- | --- |
  | Instance | 7,232 B on i386, 7,248 on 64-bit [verified: `sizeof`], including a shared 768-float noise buffer (3,072 B) |
  | Cost of a 64-frame block, this Mac [verified] | idle 0.12 µs; kick 0.77; BD SD CH OH CP 2.4; all 11 on every 16th at 125 BPM 6.3 (0.43 % of the block) |
  | Code | 10.3 KB on pi32v2 [reported: X0X performance plan]; 26.0 KB on i386 at `-O2` [verified] |
  | Tables | 13,832 B: tanh 4,104 and 19 pot curves 9,728 (`tools/gen_drum_samples.py`) [verified] |
  | Samples | **221,098 B** as int16: hh 20,250, ride 46,999 and crash 43,300 samples at 44.1 kHz [verified: the generated header] |

- **Licence.** `drum909.*` is GPL-3.0-only (X0X), from 9W9 (GPL-3.0), which
  grew out of ER-99 (GPL-3.0). The samples are ER-99's, "GPL-3.0"
  [reported: `assets/909/README.txt`]. **Neither ER-99 nor 9W9 says where
  the recordings come from** [verified: both READMEs; ER-99 added them in
  its first commit]. The real 909's cymbals are 6-bit PCM in Roland's
  ROMs, so their provenance is a risk (§6.5, R1).

**fxbus** (`dsp/fxbus*`, GPL-3.0-only; 9W9's sends and master)
[reported: `fxbus.h:2-23`]:
- a reverb: four combs and two allpasses at 12 bits;
- a tempo-synced delay, with X0X's TAPE mode (wow, flutter, saturation
  inside the loop);
- the master's distortion types and a one-knob glue compressor.

Not proposed for porting: our rack has Plate, Room, Hall, Echo, Comp and
Master Sat. TAPE is an idea for our Echo, in our own code (§7, X9).

### 2.2 The kit after the TR-808 (`dsp/drum808*`)

- **What.** Sixteen sounds on eleven tracks, with the machine's own
  switches: Tom/Conga, Rim/Claves and Clap/Maracas. 8W8's circuit models
  [reported: `drum808.h:2-19`; 8W8 README]:
  - **kick:** a bridged-T in an op-amp loop, so **Decay is loop gain**;
  - **snare:** two bridged-T shells at 173 and 336 Hz, with noise;
  - **toms and congas:** a bridged-T at Q 10.8;
  - **clap:** three bursts into a 330 ms tail;
  - **metal:** one bank of six Schmitt squares, shared by cowbell, hats and
    cymbal;
  - **cymbal:** Werner, Abel and Smith's three-path model;
  - **rim shot:** a transcription of Sonic Pi's sc808 (Yoshinosuke
    Horiuchi, Sam Aaron, MIT).
- **Per sound:** Level, Tune, Decay, Drive, Dist and two sends. Some have a
  third and fourth pot (Tone, Snappy, Attack). **Kit:** Level, Accent and
  Choke [verified: `drum808.c:1255-1310`].
- **Arithmetic** [reported: `drum808.c:1-25`]:
  - delta-form TDF-II resonators, so float follows 8W8's `double`;
  - decay constants rounded once from `double`;
  - 0.32 fixed-point phases;
  - 32-sample chunks and a 689 Hz control rate.
- **Numbers:**

  | | Value |
  | --- | --- |
  | Instance | 3,352 B, i386 and 64-bit alike [verified] |
  | Cost of a 64-frame block, this Mac [verified] | idle 0.04 µs; kick 1.9; BD SD CH OH CP 5.6; cymbal alone 2.8; all 11 on every 16th 15.0 (1.03 %) |
  | Code | 7.8 KB on pi32v2 [reported]; 35.5 KB code and 5.5 KB read-only data on i386 at `-O2` [verified] |
  | Samples | none |

- **One mutable global:** `float drum808_quiet`, the voices' silence
  threshold, which X0X's overload guard moves [verified: `drum808.c:407`].
  Two instances would share it, so it must become a field (§6.3).
- **Licence.** GPL-3.0-only (X0X), from 8W8 (GPL-3.0); the rim shot is MIT.

### 2.3 The bass after the TB-303 (`dsp/bass303*`)

- **What.** Open303's `getSample()` path, statement for statement, with
  these changes [reported: `bass303.h:2-30`]:
  - The oscillators are a polyBLEP saw and the 303 square (a tanh shaper)
    instead of Open303's mip-mapped tables.
  - It oversamples 2× (Open303 uses 4×), through a polyphase IIR halfband.
  - Control values update every 4 samples, interpolated between.
  - It goes idle after 500 ms of silence.
- **Parameters:** Devilfish ranges for Slide (2–360 ms) and AccDec
  (30–3,000 ms). Drive is Soft (tilt EQ and tanh) or RAT (an op-amp IIR
  and x / (1 + x⁴)^¼ at 2×).
- **Twelve parameters**, under our cap of fourteen (raised from twelve by
  PR #72, glide): Cutoff, Reso, EnvMod, Decay, Accent, Wave, Tune, Volume,
  Drive, DrvTyp, Slide and AccDec [verified: `bass303.c:22-35`;
  `FM1_MOD_SINK_PARAMS` 192 on `origin/main` at `275628b`].
- **Numbers:**

  | | Value |
  | --- | --- |
  | Instance | 752 B; 776 at 4× [verified] |
  | Cost of a 64-frame block, this Mac [verified] | idle 0.03 µs; an acid line with full EnvMod 2.9; with RAT drive 4.0 |
  | Lite mode | 1× oversampling saves about 12 % of the FM-1's worst case [reported: `bass303.h:154-156`] |
  | Code | 11.0 KB on pi32v2 [reported]; 16.5 KB on i386 [verified] |
  | Tables | none |

- **Licence.** `bass303.*` is GPL-3.0-only (X0X), from schwung-303
  (GPL-3.0). Its parts:
  - Open303: **MIT** (Robin Schmidt);
  - the Devilfish extensions: after jc303 (midilab, GPL-3.0);
  - the RAT model: after dm-Rat (Dave Mollen, GPL-3.0).

  So an MIT-only 303 would mean porting Open303 ourselves, without the
  Devilfish and RAT parts (§7, X3).

### 2.4 TB-3PO (`seq/tb3po*`)

**What** [verified: `tb3po.c:80-146`]: a seeded generator of 303 lines.
- For each step: a rest with probability 1 − Density; otherwise, on a beat
  the root 35 % of the time, else a random degree of the scale; an octave
  within the range; Accent and Slide by probability. A slide is never also
  an accent, and a slide into a rest is dropped.
- Six scales, 1–32 steps, a xorshift32 random stream.
- Mutate re-rolls about 25 % of the steps, from the line's running stream.
- In X0X it writes the line into the 303's own step pattern, where it can
  be edited. It does not play MIDI live.

Cost: about 2 KB of code and 80 bytes a line [verified: i386 object;
`sizeof(bpart_t)`].

Licence: GPL-3.0. It ports the Phazerville Hemisphere Suite `TB_3PO`
applet (djphazer and contributors), through schwung-tb3po.

### 2.5 The break generator (`dsp/breaks*`): read, not ported

- **What** [reported: `breaks.h`; X0X design plan]:
  - An IMA ADPCM loop (22,050 Hz; a user slot, or two built-in loops that
    X0X's own 909 renders at build time) is cut into 8 slices.
  - Each beat recombines them by Complexity, Anchor (kick and snare slices
    on beats 1 and 3), Roll, Fill, Retrig odds, Phrase length and a B loop
    on the fill bar.
  - Pitch follows tempo.
- **Numbers:** 6,272 B; about 11 ns a sample on the Mac when heard, nothing
  when unheard [verified: `sizeof`; reported: the performance plan].
- **Licence.** X0X marks the files "GPL-3.0-only here, by permission" of
  mestela. schwung-breakbeat has **no licence file**, so its code is all
  rights reserved [verified: the repository at `c6e5b94`]. Its
  `src/module.json` does say `"license": "MIT"` [verified: the same
  commit, found in review], but a metadata field without a licence text or
  copyright line is not a grant we should build on: it says what the author
  probably intends, and the request below asks the author to confirm it
  with a `LICENSE` file. That permission was given to X0X, not to us.
  **Do not port.** §5 drafts the request.
  - The upstream repository also carries well-known break recordings
    (`samples/amen*.wav` and others), which nobody can licence to us.
  - X0X ships none [reported: README.md "Breaks"].

What every wrapper must also handle:
1. **The rate.** X0X's DSP is fixed at 44,100 Hz. Our resampler only
   converts down (a ratio of 1 to 4, `fm1_resampler.h`), so it cannot take
   44,100 to the FM-1's 44,118 (R4).
2. **Call boundaries.** The 909 re-anchors its envelopes at each call, the
   808 works in 32-sample chunks and the 303 at a control rate of 4. Their
   output therefore depends on where calls split, and our hosts split
   blocks at events. To give the same output at host blocks of 1, 7 and
   64, as Drums does, the wrapper renders on a fixed grid of its own (R5).
3. Gain: §2.6.
4. `drum808_quiet` per instance.
5. Every byte of instance memory initialised, whatever the fill.

## 3. Felucca's other engines, one by one

**What they are.** Fourteen `eng_*.c`, all integer (Q15), with Felucca's
voice framework under them:
- `voice_t`: 3 phases and 8 state words;
- the track's ADSR (`voice.c` `env_tick`);
- per-block modulation (`vmod_t`);
- eight EDIT parameters per engine, which with the ADSR's four make
  twelve. That leaves two under our cap of fourteen (PR #72) for Glide and
  Voice Mode from our `engines/src/glide.h`.

**What porting needs.** A shim that implements that framework: voice
allocation, the ADSR with Felucca's curve tables (`tools/gen_tables.py`),
pitch increments at 44.1 kHz, the knob-to-parameter maps and presets. Then
each engine compiles unmodified.
- The work is in the shim (about 600 lines of `voice.c` behaviour to
  reproduce), once, plus a small adapter per engine [inferred].
- Integer code gives the same bits on every build, so native and Wasm
  parity is easy.

**Costs.** Felucca's own `tests/cpu_baseline.txt` gives host instructions a
sample, 8 notes held, cc `-O2` on a Mac, including about 286 for the idle
mix [reported]. For scale, FM6 there costs 531–1,475. Our FM6 at twelve
voices takes 0.36–0.64 % of a block on this desktop [reported:
engines/README.md].

### 3.2 Ranking: port up to four

These add sounds nothing in Lunar makes, at small cost:

1. **WHEEL**: a new sound family (organ, with percussion, click and
   rotary). Cheap, and needs no stored material.
2. **TRIO**: a character our VA engines do not have. Cheap; needs nothing
   stored.
3. **SLICE, with SAMPLE's zone and ADPCM core.**
   - It adds sample slicing, which fits the owner's drum emphasis and gives
     breaks a home while BB Gen waits.
   - The built-in BREAK takes 22 KB of flash.
   - Its source could instead be **rendered at build time by our own MIT
     Drums**, as X0X renders its built-in loops with its 909. Then the
     material is ours, not Felucca's.
   - User slots wait for the flash gate. In the simulator, uploads go to
     browser storage.
4. **PHASE**: CrispyZebra's resonant-window waves are the part of the Casio
   CZ sound that Plaits' PD does not make. Cheap.

**Next, if wanted:**
- LOFI, for the wave RAM and stepped envelope.
- NOISE, for its pitched LFSR and META tones, which are good for
  percussion.
- SAMPLE with its CC0 sets. These cost 104 KB of flash. Because the
  samples are CC0, an MIT sample player of our own could ship them in the
  MIT build.
- PHYS, for the sympathetic strings. Its DSP files are MIT.
- GRAIN, once samples exist.

**Not recommended:** ANALOG, VOICE, DRUM and DIGITAL, which overlap what
we have.

### 6.4 What each stage measures (listen-free; the owner listens later)

- **Faults:** no NaN, infinity or clip at defaults; subnormals counted in
  output and in state.
- **CPU:** a 64-frame block on the desktop, idle and dense.
- **RAM:** instance bytes at 64 and 32 bits (Wasm and i386).
- **Flash:** code and tables from the JieLi compile check, with the switch
  on, against the FM-1 budget: the app area up to about 852 KB in FM-1+VA's
  layout, of which about 386 KB was ours on 2026-10-02 (docs/11;
  `notes/2026-10-02-jieli-compile-check.md`). The chain's RAM is checked
  against 387,924 B.
- **Simulator:** native against Wasm byte-identical (parity); the layout
  sweep with 0 faults; a module rebuild whenever its inputs change.

### 6.5 Risks

- **R1. The 909 cymbal samples.**
  - ER-99 and 9W9 call them GPL-3.0 but do not say what they were
    recorded from.
  - If they come from a real TR-909, their licence is doubtful.
  - Keep them behind the switch, ask ER-99's author, or offer a
    samples-free Comet with synthesised metal: the 808 kit's bank or our
    cymbal.
- **R2. Flash: 221 KB of int16 samples** is about a quarter of the whole
  app area. 8-bit (about 110 KB) or IMA ADPCM (about 55 KB) would need an
  SNR check against int16; the originals were 6-bit.
- **R3. CPU on the FM-1.**
  - By X0X's own figures, a dense 808 is about 40 % of the CPU, a 303
    about 16 % and a dense 909 about 12 % [inferred].
  - Our 64-frame blocks, effects and sequencer come on top.
  - Wanted: an overload guard like X0X's (shed 303 oversampling first,
    then 808 tails), and measurement on the dev kit.
- **R4. The rate.**
  - X0X's units are fixed at 44,100 Hz. Our resampler cannot upsample, and
    the simulator asks for 44,118 Hz first.
  - Options (§7, X5):
    - run them unchanged at 44,118: 0.7 cent sharp, times 0.04 % short,
      the same order as X0X's own I2S error;
    - extend the resampler to ratios just below 1;
    - recompute the constants per rate.
  - Hosts much faster (48 kHz) are refused, as the Plaits engines refuse
    them.
- **R5. Output depends on call boundaries** (§2.7). A fixed internal grid
  either delays events to grid points or adds a block of latency. Moving
  the engines' anchors to absolute sample time is cleaner but changes
  vendored code.
- **R6. Gain.** Raw peaks of 2–7 at defaults; the wrappers must scale to
  our ±1 convention, and that changes the comparison to X0X by a constant.
- **R7. Subnormals in state on pi32v2.** Unknown speed. Flush to zero in
  the wrappers, or measure on the dev kit.
- **R8. The float div0 trap.** If our firmware or the SDK's start-up turns
  EMU div0 on, a hoisted float divide crashes, in these ports and in our
  own engines. Our boot must leave it off, and `audit_link.py` could
  assert that (§7, X8).
- **R9. Per-pad values cannot be read back** (state note §5, gap 1). Both
  kits need API v4's getter before a kit can be saved faithfully.
- **R10. Licence hygiene.**
  - X0X's files are GPL-3.0-only, Felucca's too, and the RAT and Devilfish
    parts are GPL. Open303 and the sc808 rim are MIT.
  - None of it may be combined with LXR code in a shared work.
  - While the switch is on, no firmware image that links JieLi's libraries
    may be shared, and the public simulator is GPL-offered.
  - The wrappers are our code, built only with the switch.
- **R11. Names.** TB-3PO, X0X, 9W9 and 8W8 appear only in credits.
- **R12. Felucca's shim** is the largest single piece of new code. A
  mistake in its ADSR or voice handling would show in all four engines.
  Test it against Felucca's own renders.
- **R13. Upstream moves fast:** X0X had 62 commits of its own from 2026-10-03 to 2026-10-05. Pin by
  commit and re-check with `UPSTREAM.md`'s hashes before each stage.
