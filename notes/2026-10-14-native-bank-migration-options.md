# Native-bank migration options — 2026-10-14

## Decision

[verified] The recorded approval for the `expanded-d9000` application layout does **not** yet authorize VM/USR bank reuse. The approval changes the application-area end and requires settings migration and rollback verification; it explicitly preserves reserved-bank boundaries. The currently admitted geometry and its verifier also preserve all raw bytes from `0xD9000` through `0xFFFFF`. No source or receipt establishes that the FM-1_092 VM or USR bank is semantically unused, nor that exporting it off-device and erasing it preserves the owner's supported stock settings/state.

[verified] A lossless external archive is an established offline mechanism, but not yet a sufficient migration gate. `tools/native/storage_backup.py` requires two identical full 1 MiB images, checks named geometry, and archives the complete baseline plus exact VM, BTIF, USR and globals-gap bytes. The retained historical-092 archive reports baseline SHA-256 `96006a51917750743d7adc68461f1289e82adc004a75bd2bbd5da40ecc44c4e4` and manifest SHA-256 `ae1bb3f601e8bdffa2980bb6b4a24ced4439ecda62619fe7f7a07cdb2d9a2923`, but explicitly leaves `backup_provenance_verified`, `semantic_migration_verified`, `physical_rollback_verified`, and `hardware_admitted` false. It is a historical snapshot, not evidence of the current device state.

The most promising physical allocation *if* ownership and migration are later proved is one 48 KiB SAVE slot in VM and one 48 KiB slot in USR. That totals 96 KiB, uses only named 4 KiB sectors, and fits inside their 64 KiB and 72 KiB FM-1_092 extents. It is not implementable with the present store contract: `firmware/native/project_store.h` describes two same-sized adjacent slots at `base + i * slot_bytes`. Supporting the separated banks requires an explicit two-extent geometry contract and a mapper that makes every read/erase/program operation stay within its assigned bank. This candidate also supports the source-bounded native SAVE limit only (43,130 bytes plus the 32-byte header fits a 48 KiB slot); it does not preserve the current generic 98,304-byte import cap, which needs 100 KiB per generation. The latter requires 204,800 bytes total and cannot fit in the 136 KiB combined VM+USR space.

- There is no reviewed VM/USR payload parser/semantic inventory in the inspected `inspect_stock_flash.py`, `raw_flash_profile.py` or storage backup tool. These tools parse descriptors and preserve opaque bytes; descriptor labels do not explain their payloads.

## Exact missing gates and artifacts

1. **Identify and characterize the bank contents offline.** Obtain the exact stock firmware/package lineage and two complete matching raw images for the target hardware/version, with retained acquisition manifest linking identity, device serial or owner inventory label, raw-file hashes, timestamps, read procedure/tool revision and independent operator/check. On offline copies, build a byte-preserving VM/USR inventory: known format signatures, version/schema, all record extents, checksums, meaningful settings/project/state fields, erased ranges and unknown bytes. If opaque records remain, preserve them and do not label those sectors unused. A stock-bank decoder or vendor/community format evidence must be independently checked against actual bytes; current descriptor parsers are insufficient.
2. **Define preservation versus migration.** Map every required value/record to the new Lunar format and prove round-trip equivalence for supported settings and stock rollback. Name any non-migrated stock feature/state and provide a recovery path that restores the exact original bytes. Keep the full raw baseline and per-bank copies in at least two owner-controlled offline locations; pin both full-image and archive-manifest hashes outside the archive. The existing retained historic archive may be an analysis input, but its provenance flag is false and it is not a fresh target baseline.
3. **Bind a new named layout/profile.** Only after byte ownership is established, specify exact sectors, bank descriptors, erase/program rules, image profile/version and reserved-region protections. Keep BTIF, the globals gap and key_mac protected unless each receives its own separate proof/decision. Build a verifier that rejects wrong identity/profile, overlap, missing archive, changed protected bytes and a scatter map outside the exact extents. The existing `expanded_flash_profile.py` must not be bypassed or relabeled.
4. **Resolve the product-capacity contract.** For two 48 KiB slots, enforce `payload <= 49,152 - 32 = 49,120` bytes and provide clear rejection for larger imports. The evidence cited in the storage-capacity work bounds native SAVE records at 43,130 bytes; that is not a generic import bound. If maintaining 98,304-byte imports and two transactional generations is required, choose a larger independently owned layout or another storage medium; VM+USR alone is mathematically insufficient.
5. **Implement and prove split transactional storage offline.** Make slot extents independent inputs (not a single contiguous base), bind `slot[0]` to exact VM sectors and `slot[1]` to exact USR sectors, and test that all operations remain in bounds. Verify generation selection, full-file validation, checksums/readback, commit-marker-last behavior and rollback to the previous SAVE when every erase/program/acknowledgement point is interrupted. Exercise both physical-bank orderings and corruption, read failure and ambiguous-generation cases. Use exact emitted candidate geometry in recovery tests.
6. **Prove whole-device recovery before any write admission.** Produce the exact rollback image and recovery procedure from the fresh full dump; verify full-image hashes and every protected range, including stock SPL/OTA/config/key data. Recovery should restore original VM/USR bytes and original stock layout exactly, and must be tested under offline torn install and torn restore at bank boundaries. A passing model is not physical recovery proof. The project's existing physical installation prerequisites remain: fresh device identity, two matching full backups, reviewed concrete image/recovery plan, and explicit hardware authorization for the planned operation. This research performed no hardware operations.

## Authorization boundary and recommended sequence

The existing phrase “Approve expanded layout with migration and rollback verification” authorizes engineering that preserves the named reserved banks while changing the application-area profile. The same proposal says bank boundaries remain unchanged, and no current note authorizes deleting or repurposing the contents of VM, BTIF, USR, the globals gap, or key_mac. Off-device copies are a necessary preservation tool, but a backup alone does not establish that the original feature/state can be migrated or safely surrendered.

Before any implementation that changes reserved bank descriptors or writes project data there, obtain a narrowly scoped owner decision that names: (a) target stock baseline(s), at minimum FM-1_092; (b) exact VM and/or USR extents to reclaim; (c) permission to retain the complete original bytes in the verified external recovery archive and overwrite those on-device extents; (d) whether native SAVE has a 49,120-byte maximum and generic imports above it must be rejected; and (e) acceptance of a future staged physical install only after the listed offline proof and separately reviewed recovery plan. This task does not request or imply permission for a device operation.

Recommended next step is offline characterization of the historical 092 bank copies and exact firmware lineage, followed by an owner-facing sector/cap proposal. Do not alter source or the existing profile until that characterization demonstrates which exact sectors contain only state that can be migrated or safely restored. If no bank can be shown reclaimable while preserving required state, retain the current expanded profile and seek capacity outside these opaque reservations; compiler savings or the app's unused tail do not create ownership of them.
