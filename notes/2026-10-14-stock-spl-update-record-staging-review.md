# Stock SPL update-record and RAM-loader staging review — 2026-10-14

## Finding

[verified] The recorded FM-1_092 profile has no presently justified flash extent for staging the maintained Felucca/Dinghy RAM loader and its power-loss record while preserving stock-owned VM/BTIF/USR state. Its current stock-OTA bootstrap constants place loader bytes at `[0xE0000,0xE5000)` and the 112-byte update record at `0xE4F00`; both are inside the FM-1_092 VM reservation `[0xD9000,0xE9000)`. The existing historical archive has erased sectors in the middle of VM, but two VM sectors contain non-FF data, and the erased run is not ownership or allocator evidence. Reusing even the all-FF sector at `0xE4000` is therefore not justified.

[verified] The pinned stock SPL's main control flow does not unconditionally dispatch a flash update record on ordinary XIP boot. The SPL copies an 80-byte RAM record from `0x01C7FD88`, validates its CRC and nonzero signature/field, and on the valid-record path follows its +72 flash-record pointer. With an invalid RAM record and successful ordinary application selection, it branches around the trailing-flash-record search. The prior byte-verified trace documents this at [the final SPL contract](2026-10-11-stock-spl-final-contract.md#what-the-earlier-indirect-callback-actually-selects), based on the identical pinned V15 and FM-1_092 SPL hash.

[reported: Felucca implementation and prior SPL analysis] The record format is `UPDATA_PARM`: CRC16 in bytes 0–1, fields including `0x5441` at +6, and a flash-record pointer at +72. The familiar trailing-record slots are 256 bytes before each 4 KiB boundary in `[0x93000,0xFC000)`. This scan range is reported by the earlier Felucca community/source review (`notes/2026-10-05-community-repos.md:239,409`) and is also the range Felucca's loader uses to remove matching records (`reference/dinghy/firmware/loader/ldr_core.c:91-102`). The latest committed SPL trace confirms the conditional search and signature/CRC test, but does not itself publish the loop bounds; the scan interval should remain labeled reported until the original full vendor listing is re-opened and the loop's boundary comparisons are rechecked. No candidate valid 80-byte `0x5441` record was found in the archived FM-1_092 flash image (`build/stock-spl-final-contract-review.md:73`); that says nothing about the inherited RAM mailbox.

## Why the existing path cannot simply be relocated

The stock-OTA-like Felucca staging code erases five 4 KiB sectors at `0xE0000..0xE4FFF`, writes/decompresses the loader there, and places the persistent record at `0xE4F00` (`firmware/third_party/dinghy/src/ota.c:24-29,303-339,363-389`). Its `FELUCCA_OTA_RAMONLY` switch only omits the persistent record; its own comment says that power loss leaves the staged loader behind and cannot resume (`ota.c:378-382`). It does not make the overwritten VM sectors safe.

The loader then writes app data only in its fixed `[0x4000,0x93000)` range, and its completion cleanup clears the RAM mailbox and scans/erases matching records at 4 KiB-boundary-minus-256 slots in `[0x93000,0xFC000)` (`reference/dinghy/firmware/loader/ldr_core.c:31-34,91-103,117-180`; `loader.c:19-20,70-73,108-115`). That interval intersects FM-1_092 VM, BTIF and USR. Widening the app limit to `0xD9000` without rewriting both staging and record-discovery/cleanup rules would corrupt protected state. The proposed application/profile extent is `[0x04000,0xD9000)`; geometry code rejects overlap and preserves the suffix exactly (`tools/jieli/expanded_flash_profile.py:12-29,40-60`).

A native app could theoretically copy a small updater into RAM and branch to it, but the reviewed source has no already-proven app-to-RAM loader contract that also provides restart-safe persistence. The stock SPL callback path requires a valid 80-byte inherited record plus a flash record pointer; the currently pinned stock path validates/decompresses that referenced record before calling its RAM entry (`2026-10-11-stock-spl-final-contract.md:67-85`). `ota_commit()` writes both the RAM mailbox and persistent flash record (`firmware/third_party/dinghy/src/ota.c:363-389`). A RAM-only callback can avoid a flash record only for the current warm transition; it cannot supply the loader after loss of power or after the app sectors have been partly replaced. The stock OTA staging reservation is in VM, and the historical stock archive's all-FF observation does not establish an independently stock-owned recovery slot.

The app's `.ram_text` is a flash-operation code region, not an 862 KiB image buffer (`firmware/native/app.ld:8-34`). A streaming loader can fit in tens of KiB, as the existing Dinghy loader demonstrates, but that alone does not solve how to persistently enter it after an interrupted overwrite. The existing physical USB_KEY/ROM recovery route remains a separate recovery plan, not proof that a browser-started transaction is self-resuming.

## Recommended next implementation

Do not adapt the stock OTA stage/record address or erase `0xE4000` based on its erased bytes. First do a tightly bounded re-analysis of the original pinned 14,384-byte SPL binary/disassembly to recover the exact trailing scan start/end, stride, signature/CRC offsets and pointer range checks, and compare both recorded FM-1_092 backups for any matching records. Keep the full listing and parser output private; retain only hashes, offsets and byte-verified control-flow excerpts in the note.

Then identify a permitted durable staging allocation from a version-matched stock allocator/format analysis, with an exact export/migration/rollback proof for any bytes reused. Until both gates close, the existing stock-SPL handoff cannot be used as a safe restartable browser bootstrap for the expanded-d9000 profile. If a future product path uses a separate RAM launch, it still needs a retained independent recovery/bootstrap mechanism that works after application-sector damage; do not claim a RAM-only warm handoff meets that need.

## Evidence and limits

Inputs were repository source, the committed byte-verified stock-SPL trace and the prior private archive characterization. The archive is historical and not a fresh current-device dump. The full SPL disassembly and raw backup are not present in this worktree at the paths recorded by the older receipt, so this task did not newly reproduce the scan-loop bounds. No stock SDK allocator/VM parser was available in the inspected paths. This review issued no USB, MIDI, flash, power, reboot, or device command, and made no source, package, or hardware change. It establishes a design blocker, not hardware recovery, storage admission, or browser installability.
