# FM-1_092 VM/USR ownership: source-matched review — 2026-10-15

## Finding

The retained data and source establish **where** FM-1_092 declares VM and USR, but do not establish which stock application components allocate, read, or rewrite their records. I found no version-matched FM-1_092 VM/USR decoder or allocator in this checkout. The exact stock package model preserves these areas as opaque payloads; it does not interpret them. Consequently, no byte-preserving on-device migration into the proposed storage48 map can presently be specified or verified.

The candidate map has 34 sectors of combined VM+USR geometry. Two 12-sector SAVE slots use 24 sectors; the current OTA stage reserves five more, leaving five sectors (20 KiB). The retained archive has 40,813 non-`0xFF` bytes across the complete VM and USR banks. That exceeds the entire five-sector remainder by 20,333 bytes before archive metadata. The 33,123 non-FF bytes inside the *proposed slot extents* also exceed the remainder by 12,643 bytes. Therefore uncompressed raw-byte-preserving relocation does not fit. A bounded follow-up measured lossless raw-DEFLATE that does fit this exact archive; see “Follow-up: opaque-byte compression capacity” below. Neither byte count proves which contents are live, and compressed archive capacity does not prove a safe way to create or restore the archive.

External archival is already byte-preserving and feasible: `tools/native/storage_backup.py` extracts the full image and named opaque ranges from matching inputs. It is a recovery copy, not an on-device migration or proof that stock settings/features can be restored after the old sectors are reassigned. Until stock semantics are resolved, keep VM, BTIF and USR unchanged and keep SAVE disarmed.

## Exact geometry and source responsibilities

`tools/jieli/raw_flash_profile.py` defines the recorded FM-1_092 descriptor map: VM `[0xD9000,0xE9000)` (64 KiB), BTIF `[0xE9000,0xEA000)` (4 KiB), USR `[0xEA000,0xFC000)` (72 KiB), and `key_mac` `[0xFF000,0x100000)` (4 KiB). These are descriptor-derived boundaries, not decoder output or proof of ownership within a bank. `tools/jieli/model_092_app_replacement.py` requires the decoded FWSC child order `app.bin`, `cfg_tool.bin`, VM, PRCT, BTIF, USR and preserves non-app payloads against its pinned reference. It has no VM/USR record interpretation.

The known storage-path conflict is concrete but belongs to the open-native Dinghy implementation, not the proprietary stock app. `firmware/third_party/dinghy/src/ota.c` stages its loader by erasing `[0xE0000,0xE5000)` and writes its 112-byte `UPDATA_PARM` at `0xE4F00`; both addresses are inside the FM-1_092 VM descriptor. The retained SPL analysis describes conditional recovery-record scanning, while `reference/dinghy/firmware/loader/ldr_core.c` removes matching records in the broader `[0x93000,0xFC000)` region. That open loader's cleanup range intersects VM, BTIF and USR. Neither implementation reveals what the FM-1_092 stock app stores in those ranges, and neither can be silently treated as its stock allocator.

The only stock application call trace presently recorded is in `notes/2026-10-11-sdk-diagnostic-review.md`, which explicitly analyzes a different V15 artifact identified as FM-1_009. It reports that the analyzed app retains `syscfg_file_init`, has no direct retained `syscfg_vm_init` call, and contains VM busy/idle callbacks that inspect RAM. That does not establish the 092 app's startup calls or bank semantics. The generic SDK header `source-sdk/include_lib/driver/device/vm.h` (retained in the earlier review environment, not present as a source tree in this checkout) documents `vm_init(device,start,length,mode)` and says initialization occurs each power-up; the implementation is closed, and no 092 application callsite/address arguments are available. Absence of a literal address in the old disassembly search is not proof of no computed or indirect access (`notes/2026-10-14-rollback-stage-last-cutpoint-review.md`).

## Retained archive evidence (privacy-safe)

The only bank-byte evidence used here is the existing ignored local archive `build/native-storage-backup-20261013/private-stock092/`, bound by its prior manifest to historical baseline SHA-256 `96006a51917750743d7adc68461f1289e82adc004a75bd2bbd5da40ecc44c4e4` and manifest SHA-256 `ae1bb3f601e8bdffa2980bb6b4a24ced4439ecda62619fe7f7a07cdb2d9a2923`. No bytes, presets, names, or private payload contents are reproduced.

`notes/2026-10-14-stock-bank-characterization.md` records the manifest-matched extents and aggregate sector scan:

- VM: sectors `[0xD9000,0xDA000)` and `[0xE8000,0xE9000)` contain 142 and 213 non-FF bytes; the fourteen intervening sectors are all FF.
- USR: `[0xEA000,0xF2000)` is eight fully non-FF sectors; `[0xF2000,0xFA000)` is eight all-FF sectors; `[0xFA000,0xFB000)` has 4,096 non-FF bytes (11 distinct values) and `[0xFB000,0xFC000)` has 3,594.
- Aggregate across full banks: 40,813 non-FF bytes. Within the candidate slot extents, the count is 33,123; the 7,690 non-FF bytes in `[0xF7000,0xFC000)` are outside the candidate slots. Sector `[0xFA000,0xFB000)` is fully non-FF (with 11 distinct byte values), followed by 3,594 non-FF bytes in `[0xFB000,0xFC000)`. These counts describe byte values only and do not identify live records. In particular, the erased USR run `[0xF2000,0xFA000)` lies inside the region the existing cutpoint review flags as unresolved for stock-app readiness.

The storage48 candidate map in `notes/2026-10-14-storage48-migration-plan.md` assigns A to seven VM sectors, four VM sectors after the OTA window, and one USR sector; B to twelve USR sectors; and leaves five USR sectors. It therefore does not preserve the original bytes in the mapped sectors. Even placing just the 33,123 non-FF bytes already present in the proposed slot extents into the five remaining sectors is impossible by raw-byte count alone (33,123 > 20,480). The candidate also crosses named VM/USR descriptor boundaries and needs a scatter-extent storage API; the current `firmware/native/project_store.h` represents adjacent equal-size slots. The five sectors `[0xF7000,0xFC000)` left by the proposed map themselves contain 7,690 non-`0xFF` baseline bytes. An archive placed there would overwrite bytes it is meant to preserve. A precomputed off-device archive may fit arithmetically, but safe first publication still needs a separately protected recovery source/route or a proven interruption-safe staging protocol.

## Concrete next implementation/evidence gate

Do not implement a sector-reassignment writer from the current ledger. The next useful work is a **version-matched offline stock-state export and relocation proof**, using the exact FM-1_092 app/package lineage and the retained pair as inputs:

1. Obtain an exact 092 application/loader image, its matching map/symbols or reproducible disassembly, and the corresponding VM implementation/format evidence. Verify identity and hashes against the archived baseline/package; do not substitute the FM-1_009 trace or Dinghy's independent allocator.
2. Trace actual 092 startup/config code into VM and USR APIs, resolve the descriptor handles/ranges and on-flash record/index format, then decode the archived VM/USR copies into a privacy-preserving inventory of record types, extents, references, checksums, and unknown records. Keep unknown records raw and migration-blocking.
3. If the format supports relocation, prototype a host-only exporter/importer that relocates complete records plus metadata, verifies checksums and references, and reconstructs exact old-bank bytes for rollback. Test against both archived matching images and preserved stock package components. If no matching implementation or complete record graph is obtainable, keep the banks reserved; archival bytes alone cannot justify erasure.
4. Only after that evidence, make a new explicit sector map with OTA stage/record and cleanup/SPL record scans accounted for, fault-test migration and byte-exact restoration offline, and request review of the precise extents. This note grants no device-write or bank-reuse authorization.

The source gap is therefore precise: **there is no matched 092 stock VM/USR reader/allocator and no exportable record schema in the available source**. The most direct falsifier would be a matching FM-1_092 firmware/source artifact that exposes those callsites and layout. Without it, the measured 33,123-byte displaced extent cannot be retained uncompressed in the proposed five-sector slack; compression can preserve exact bytes for this capture, but does not solve stock semantics, first-publication safety, or restore transport.

## Follow-up: opaque-byte compression capacity (2026-10-14)

I tested whether compressing the *complete physical opaque byte extents* could preserve a rollback copy in the five-sector (20,480-byte) remainder. Inputs came only from the two already-retained, hash-matching 1 MiB backups under `scratch/archived-092-recovery/`; their whole-image SHA-256 is `96006a51917750743d7adc68461f1289e82adc004a75bd2bbd5da40ecc44c4e4`. `tools/native/storage_backup.py` defines the complete concatenation as VM `[0xD9000,0xE9000)` plus USR `[0xEA000,0xFC000)`, leaving BTIF `[0xE9000,0xEA000)` separate. Recomputed VM and USR component hashes matched both this raw-image pair and the archived `vm.bin`/`usr.bin` files byte-for-byte.

The complete VM+USR stream is 139,264 bytes, SHA-256 `ece60f1ad121619b773993dbf5f6667303c7edbe8e02e1e97cb643c4a07a11db`. The proposed overwritten slot extents are VM `[0xD9000,0xE0000)` + `[0xE5000,0xE9000)` and USR `[0xEA000,0xEB000)` + `[0xEB000,0xF7000)`: 98,304 bytes, SHA-256 `024e470af997c137fd5d58a18207a253da0bba1ad75f50bceaf0d1f13fd2326e`. These hashes identify inputs only; no bank bytes are included in this note.

| Exact archived bytes | Existing `fm1_deflate` fixed-Huffman output | zlib raw DEFLATE, `wbits=-12`, level 6 | Level 6 + current 1,181-byte archive manifest size | Space left in 20,480 B after level 6 + manifest |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Complete VM+USR (139,264 B) | 21,492 B (does not fit) | 17,957 B | 19,138 B | 1,342 B |
| Proposed overwritten extents (98,304 B) | 20,922 B (does not fit) | 17,456 B | 18,637 B | 1,843 B |

For comparison, zlib raw DEFLATE at level 9 produced 17,924 B and 17,426 B respectively, 33–30 bytes smaller than the repository's existing zlib level-6 choice. The zlib level-6 output SHA-256 values were `e676d5348a3e9c2bb41ffd9990ab367b536fc2bdbf96fbbb8dfaeb698229b39a` (full VM+USR) and `592d72d88084e7d944e8ddc11194046a7ae529f2fb2656ea0f065cc38b0922c0` (candidate extents). The installed Python zlib compile/runtime versions were both 1.2.12. I linked a temporary host harness against the repository's actual `engines/state/fm1_deflate.c`: its current `fm1_inflate` implementation decoded both level-6 and level-9 `wbits=-12` streams back to the exact input bytes, consumed the complete declared compressed range, and reported completion. The repository compressor's fixed-Huffman output was also measured and round-tripped through the same source. Temporary harness/binaries and compressed payloads were not retained.

**Capacity result:** an exact archived FM-1_092 opaque-bank copy is arithmetically feasible within 20,480 bytes using the already-used host zlib raw-DEFLATE format plus metadata no larger than the existing 1,181-byte manifest. This is a measured one-image result, not a guaranteed bound for other devices, firmware versions, settings, or future sector contents. A first-install preflight must compress the two matching captured inputs, account for the actual versioned header/index/hash bytes and alignment, and refuse before any mutation if the complete encoded archive exceeds the admitted extent. The current in-firmware fixed-Huffman compressor does **not** fit either measured input in 20,480 bytes.

This result makes no semantic migration claim. `fm1_inflate` is an existing bounded project-state decoder (4 KiB window); I verified codec round-trip on the host, but did not build an archive reader, validate the Pi32v2 runtime cost for that use, or implement a flash-recovery path which reads the archive and restores the original VM/USR ranges. The existing storage backup utility stores uncompressed opaque ranges and has no such archive format or restore command. Publishing the compressed archive into the five remaining sectors would also overwrite 7,690 non-FF source bytes. Before any such publication, those bytes need a separate protected recovery copy and the transaction needs a verified interruption path. A successful readback/hash after writing would not protect a power-loss window during initial archive publication. Compression feasibility alone does not make rollback operational or authorize sector reassignment.

The compression scan also found an arithmetic error in the prior sector summary above and in `notes/2026-10-14-stock-bank-characterization.md`: sector `[0xFA000,0xFB000)` has **4,096** non-`0xFF` bytes (only 11 distinct byte values), not 11 non-FF bytes. Thus archived USR has 40,458 non-FF bytes; full VM+USR have 40,813; the candidate slot extents have 33,123; and the unassigned tail `[0xF7000,0xFC000)` has 7,690, not 3,605. These are byte-value counts only. The compression test used exact raw bytes and hashes, not those density counts.
