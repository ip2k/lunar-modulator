**Conclusion:** recovery is unproven. The latest result establishes that the FPGA completed its programmed schedule and released its output enables. It does not establish that the FM1 received valid electrical levels, accepted a key, calibrated its oscillator, or executed its ROM. ## Prioritized findings

### 1. High priority — the 1 kΩ drive path has an unresolved loaded-low margin

**Verified design:** the r1.4 schematic places R58 = 1 kΩ between FPGA D+ monitor pin N4 and the target bus, and R57 = 1 kΩ on D−/P3. The custom platform changes these monitor resources from inputs into LVCMOS33 bidirectional pins, with internal pulls disabled. The repeat profile drives both lines push-pull during KEY and D+ push-pull during PWM. The FPGA input samples are on the FPGA side of these resistors.

**Conditional calculation:** if the target enables a 1.5 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V while the FPGA drives D+ low through 1 kΩ, the ideal target-side voltage is `3.3 × 1 / (1 + 1.5) = 1.32 V`. That is between the USB3343 full-speed receiver's specified low/high limits, 0.8/2.0 V. This is a credible failure mechanism for falling-edge calibration and for PHY observations. It is **not a measured FM1 voltage**, and those USB3343 limits are not verified AC791N ROM GPIO thresholds. Pull-up strength, activation time, target loading, and real voltages remain unknown.

The series resistance limits contention current; it does not eliminate contention or guarantee valid low levels. A target driving both lines low for an ACK can oppose the ongoing push-pull key. Neither fault=0 nor a local output-enable bit detects that electrical condition. Do not short/reduce the resistors, increase drive, or change polarity on this calculation alone.

### 2. High priority — passing self-test and DONE answer narrower questions than recovery

**Verified:** the disconnected four-state test requires the PHY to report K, J, SE0 and SE1 matching the FPGA stimulus. This exercises local routing and reception without the FM1 load. Pad-input values are recorded for diagnostics but do not determine acceptance. `bank6_3v3_confirmed` is tied to 1 from the board design, not measured by an ADC.

The repeat controller deliberately ignores ACK/RXCMD for progression. It counts 3,000 words and two seconds of PWM per cycle, and completes after 16 cycles. Thus DONE is equally possible with an absent, unpowered, rejecting, or responsive target. The latest terminal receipt has mask 15, state 7, 16 cycles, 3,000 final-cycle words, 2,000,000 PWM microseconds, no reported PHY fault, and OEs zero. Those are coherent digital results.

The final frozen ring alternates J/SE0 every 500 µs during the last PWM. Its terminal J-like entry has **no new RXCMD event** and occurs as the controller releases its last driven high. It is not evidence of a target pull-up after release. Later live status is SE0. The active sweep is non-atomic and spans seconds while a 32-entry ring wraps; apparent jumps in such a sweep are not missing physical pulses. The stable terminal sweep is the usable reconstruction.

### 3. High priority — Pico-inspired is not electrically or temporally equivalent to Pico

**Verified at RTL:** `0x16ef` is sent MSB first (`0001011011101111`), with data on D− and D+ low 10 µs/high 10 µs. Data is stable before the rising edge. This agrees with the strongest primary descriptions. I found no source-supported reason to reverse bit order, clock polarity, or D+/D−.

| Property | Current Cynthion profile | czietz reference / remaining uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Word | 16 bits at 50 kHz; 320 µs | Same requested SPI frequency and bytes |
| Interword idle | Explicit 160 µs with both OEs off, including after final word | Interpreter/SPI setup delay unmeasured; deinit does not establish high impedance |
| PWM | D+ driven low 500 µs then high 500 µs, exactly 2 s | Requested 1 kHz/50%; actual first/last phase unmeasured |
| D− during PWM | Released | Gist changes only D+ to PWM; retained D− state unmeasured |
| Repetition | 16 cycles, 55.04 s | Infinite loop; gist reports power-on alignment can fail |

One Cynthion cycle is `3000 × (320 + 160) µs + 2 s = 3.44 s`; the key phase is 1.44 s. A released gap is not a driven-low idle. Its voltage depends on target and PHY loading. In inspected MicroPython RP2 v1.19.1 source, SPI has no deinit callback; generic deinit does nothing in that case. This disproves an assumption that the API name guarantees pin release, but it does not pin the firmware or waveform of the successful Pico trials.

**Plausible, unproven timing failures:** an early accepted key may encounter more 50 kHz key clocks and released gaps during the reported calibration window, rather than clean 1 ms falling-edge periods. The reported approximately one-second calibration timeout could expire before the planned PWM starts. Conversely, an actual CPU reset during the two-second PWM may miss a short boot-time key window; later cycles do not necessarily reopen it. WL82/FM1 listening-window length and internal reset timing are not established. The signal called SOF here is a calibration square wave, not decoded USB SOF packets.

### 4. Medium priority — PHY setup is coherent, but runtime isolation claims need limits

**Verified:** r1.4 uses N4=D+, P3=D− for the monitor pins. The PHY's crossed connection is separately compensated by register 0x39=0x06, matching the pinned vendor platform. GPIO bit order should not be swapped merely because the PHY requires that setting. The initializer checks identity, writes and reads back 0x04=0x49, 0x0a=0, 0x32=0, and 0x39=0x06 before enabling drive. These select full-speed non-driving operation and clear the relevant pull/charger/VBUS controls. The ready receipt supports successful execution of this readback path; it does not separately retain a raw 0x39 byte.

After startup, COMPLETE does not periodically verify these registers or monitor connector voltage. An injected `phy_fault` test proves the core responds to that input; it does not prove the live PHY detects every later reset, brownout, clamp or configuration fault. OEs zero removes FPGA drive but leaves the PHY, ESD structures and routing attached. The datasheet's approximately 2 MΩ pulldowns are specified for Low Power Mode; that is a warning against assuming infinite impedance, not a measured value for the active non-driving setup.

**Operational boundary:** TARGET A and TARGET C data lines remain connected through the shared target path/common-mode chokes. `target_c_vbus_en=0` isolates a power source, not host data. Keeping TARGET C physically unplugged during keying is an operator interlock. There is no FPGA-controlled data switch or host-presence interlock here. This is a real contention risk if the procedure is violated, not evidence that it was violated in the latest run.

### 5. Medium priority — concrete ACK telemetry and documentation defects

Terminal register 0x0e bit 1 snapshots `debug_ack_confirm`, a transient predicate qualified by `us_tick`, rather than the persistent `ack_confirmed_latched` flag. It cannot reliably answer whether a candidate occurred earlier. The history already records that latched flag. Historical attempt 5 and 6 reports also incorrectly decode `0x18d` as ACK-confirm=1: bit 1 is **zero**; the value is receiver-live=1, ACK-confirm=0, ticks=99. WAIT_DP and the history latch still establish a local candidate transition, but neither establishes a genuine ROM ACK.

**Concrete fix recommendation:** expose the persistent candidate flag in terminal telemetry, explicitly version/document the field semantics, and add a production-60-MHz terminal-snapshot regression. Correct the two historical decodes without erasing their provenance. This affects diagnosis, not the latest repeat waveform, which bypasses ACK-based progression.

A separate documentation error labels history key phase as USB clock cycles. It increments once per microsecond and is a microsecond phase index. Correct that label and any parser assumptions; no waveform change follows.

### 6. Medium priority — the digital tests share the assumptions under investigation

The repeat tests force permissions, feed expected RXCMD states, and compare output bits with the same nominal bit/gap/PWM schedule. Most use a reduced 1 MHz clock and short counts. The fake PHY stores and returns register bytes; it cannot validate analog non-driving behavior. Protocol tests model a pulled-up bus driven low as a perfect binary zero, excluding the 1 kΩ/1.5 kΩ divider. The behavioural ROM explicitly models reported behaviour of other JieLi families, not observed AC791N silicon. These tests are useful for deterministic scheduling, bounds, and interlocks. They are not an independent oracle for target reception or ROM semantics.

## Causal assessment and ranking

The initial failure and the failed recovery can have different causes. Interrupted flashing is a plausible cause of the initial nonbooting application/boot chain. A separate recovery electrical or timing problem can coexist with an intact mask ROM. Neither old `-71` events nor present no-attach proves ROM entry or permanent silicon damage.

Ranked by present explanatory support and diagnostic priority, without numerical probabilities:

1. **Boot/flash state disrupted by the reported interruption.** Best-supported explanation of the initial dark unit, but exact flash region and current execution state are unknown. No original flash contents or CPU trace proves it.
2. **Loaded recovery electrical failure.** The concrete 1 kΩ topology makes this the highest-value recoverability uncertainty to test. Clamping, bad low margin, cable/connector trouble, or target loading can coexist with flawless FPGA counters.
3. **No appropriate reset/listening window, or protocol/calibration mismatch.** The battery, unmeasured CPU reset, finite KEY/PWM alternation, and unmeasured Pico idles leave this open. Correct switch direction removes one error but does not measure reset.
4. **CPU supply/reset/clock fault or target USB pull-up/path fault.** Plausible, currently unmeasured. External VBUS and charging are insufficient to rank these internal possibilities separately.
5. **Full-speed host/analyzer path failure as an additional confounder.** Still open because only Low speed passed; cannot by itself explain the blank display and earlier direct-host failure.

Permanent SoC damage, eFuse damage, a proven wrong clock polarity, a proven good connector waveform, and successful ROM calibration are unsupported claims. No retained evidence selects one as the cause.

## ONE recommended next experiment

**Capture the loaded D+/D− waveform at the FM1 end of the cable during one unchanged, finite repeat attempt, with VBUS recorded alongside it.** This is a proposal for the coordinator to execute separately after bench authorization. It adds measurement, not a new recovery algorithm. A direct-host-only scope capture would require fewer steps, but would leave the main custom-drive uncertainty unresolved. A full-speed peripheral control would validate transport but not key delivery. This test has greater diagnostic value before opening the FM1 or retuning the waveform.

**Setup:** retain the exact pinned repeat image/profile, source pins, CONTROL supply, and cable configuration. Insert a verified transparent USB 2.0 pass-through breakout immediately at the FM1 connector, preserving VBUS/GND/data and the existing USB-C CC arrangement. Identify D+ and D− by verified wiring, not silkscreen guesses. Place CH1 on target-side D+, CH2 on D−, CH3 on VBUS. Use high-impedance 10× probes, DC coupling, short ground springs to USB GND; use differential/isolated probes if the grounding arrangement is uncertain. No internal FM1 probes, added pull resistors, alternate power source, or shorted series resistors. TARGET C stays physically unplugged throughout GPIO drive.

**Acquisition:** use an analog scope, not logic thresholds alone. Capture from before USER2 through at least 100 ms after terminal release, with at least 1 MS/s per data channel (ten samples per 10 µs half-bit) and enough memory/streaming for the approximately 56-second interval. This is about 56 million samples per data channel; confirm capability before involving the owner. A simultaneous finer segmented capture at ≥10 MS/s is preferable for edge detail if supported. At 1 MS/s the test establishes plateaus and gross timing, not USB full-speed signal integrity or nanosecond edge compliance. Record absolute high/low plateaus, any clamp plateau, 10/10 µs key timing, 160 µs released gaps, first KEY→PWM transition, 500/500 µs PWM, D− idle, and line settling/persistence after release. Save raw samples, probe scaling, fixture photo, and synchronized operator switch/USER timestamps. If the scope cannot retain those regions in one run, prepare a supported acquisition plan first; do not substitute another uninstrumented retry.

**Single run:** perform the existing disconnected preflight with both target ports empty. Require mask 15, READY, OEs zero and no reported PHY fault. Attach the LEFT/OFF FM1 and probes as prepared; arm the capture. USER2 starts the finite sequence; move the switch RIGHT/ON as in the existing procedure, documenting the actual time. Let the single bounded sequence finish unless a stop condition occurs. Record terminal registers and at least 100 ms of released-line behavior. A physical switch timestamp is still not a CPU-reset witness. Any later host handoff must wait for confirmed OE release; no loader or flash operation follows this diagnostic.

| Observation | Supported interpretation | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| VBUS absent or collapses | External supply/connector path problem; battery/CPU state still unknown | Stop recovery attempts and resolve that path first |
| Missing/reversed key, clamped levels, or wrong timing at FM1 | Delivery differs from RTL; nominal counters did not validate the connector | Localize wiring/load/drive before changing protocol |
| PWM low stalls near the conditional divider value while a pull-up appears | Supports divider-limited low under load; FM1 recognition remains unknown | Separately review the electrical driver; do not merely lower R58 |
| Clean key and target-side activity in released gaps, then abnormal PWM/handoff | Shows target-side electrical activity; its source and relation to calibration remain unknown | Preserve trace and investigate the specific transition |
| Clean waveform, no target-dependent response, released lines remain low | Still compatible with rejected key/listening window and inactive/reset-held CPU | Stop blind repetitions; next dependency is a verified CPU-rail/reset witness, requiring separate access planning |
| Persistent released D+ high after settling, but no valid descriptor | Pull-up/attach evidence, with calibration or full-speed transport unresolved | Qualify the full-speed host path before interpreting packet absence |
| Valid bootloader descriptor after a separately permitted handoff | Strong recovery-entry evidence | Stop and preserve evidence; no flash/write authorization follows |

Use <0.8 V for lows and >2.0 V for highs as conservative USB3343 observation screens, while explicitly retaining the unknown FM1 ROM threshold. A transient high on output release is not an independently driven pull-up; require persistence after settling, with Cynthion OEs off and TARGET C absent, and report its duration. Even that establishes external bias, not a ROM identity.

**Stop conditions:** failed disconnected preflight; a reported fault or unexpected drive after terminal completion; unexpected heating; an uncertain probe ground or fixture connection; sustained electrical levels outside the verified equipment/device limits; or acquisition failure that makes the run non-diagnostic. The coordinator must define and verify the existing safe abort/release action before arming. No automatic retry, live reconfiguration, polarity swap, resistor modification, or host connection while GPIO drive remains enabled. A fault terminates this experiment and requires review, not another button press.

**Explicit residual limit:** this external measurement cannot conclusively distinguish an unpowered/reset-held CPU from a running ROM that rejects the key when neither produces a response. A definitive separation needs a verified internal CPU-rail/reset/clock test point or another independent execution witness; the packet supplies neither. Do not infer those signals from USB VBUS.

## Completion and tooling limits

The requested read-only audit is complete and these findings are now being persisted at the owner's request. No implementation fix or experiment was executed. Concrete changes identified are terminal candidate telemetry, two historical decodes, and a documentation unit correction. A post-release diagnostic observation window would improve evidence, but is an enhancement, not a proven recovery fix. Changes to 0x39, pin polarity, resistance, PWM timing or protocol should await measured evidence.

Rarefaction and Serena were checked but selected the separate primary checkout, where the reviewed files were unavailable. Their shared selection was not changed during parallel work. This audit used the exact pinned worktree, direct source inspection, independent offline tests, artifact hashing and raw-capture decoding; it does not claim successful semantic-tool coverage or independent physical reproduction.
