# 2026-11-10 — M5 on the FM-1: the image loaded and was started, then the chip reset to V15

Milestone M5 of docs/18, run by the runbook docs/19 on the owner's FM-1 with
the owner at the bench. **Result: the RAM load and the jump worked; the image
showed nothing.** About 3.9 s after the jump the FM-1 reset and booted stock
V15. No test pattern, no LED blinks. The flash was identical before and after
the run, and the unit identifies as `FM-1_015` afterwards.

## What it answers (docs/19 §6)

- **The power switch cuts the SoC fully with the battery in.** With the
  FM-1 off, the transporter reported `uboot=0 v15=0` (steps 8 and 14), and
  the next power-on came up in the ROM again [verified].
- **The transporter after an unanswered `jump`:** the ROM's USB device
  stayed attached until the chip reset, 3.89 s after the jump; the
  transporter then fell back to host mode and waited. `status` worked
  normally afterwards [verified].
- **Whether the image runs:** not settled. The image never drew or blinked.

## Why it showed nothing

A read-only diagnosis on 2026-11-10 (three investigators and a judge, Opus;
no hardware) ranked these. Spot-checked claims are marked [verified].

1. **The reset is the mask ROM's own ~4 s watchdog, which `m5.bin` never
   clears.** Likelihood: high [inferred].
   - The entry was cold: the power switch cuts the SoC (above), so the ROM
     armed the watchdog itself.
   - FM-1-transporter's protocol notes say "The ROM runs a 4 s watchdog"
     [reported: FM-1-transporter `docs/PROTOCOL.md`].
   - Nothing in `firmware/m5/` touches the watchdog [verified].
2. **The watchdog may also explain the blank screen.** Likelihood: moderate
   [inferred]. The image turns the panel's display on (`0x29`) only after
   the whole 240×240 frame is sent [verified:
   `firmware/display/m5_pattern.c` sends `0x29` after the pixel loop;
   `lunar_panel_setup` in `firmware/display/panel_probe.c` sends only `0x11`
   and the init table]. The LED blink loop starts after that [verified:
   `firmware/m5/main.c`].
   - The frame time on silicon has never been measured.
   - TIMER4 is bound assuming a 40 MHz oscillator, where the kit points to
     24 MHz, so every wait runs about 1.67 times long [inferred:
     `notes/2026-11-09-m6-image.md`].
   - If setup, waits and 115,200 pixel bytes took more than about 3.9 s at
     the cold ROM's clock, the watchdog fired before display-on and before
     the first blink. The backlight could have been on over a display-off
     panel without being obvious.
   - This corrects the first draft of this note, which assumed the pattern
     would appear "in its first few hundred milliseconds".
3. **A stop or fault before the backlight.** Likelihood: low [inferred]. The
   ROM's jump to `0x1c02000` already worked on this unit in Part A (the
   loader answered `info`), and the entry stub is proven on the kit.
4. **Both the PA2 backlight role and the LED lines are wrong.** Likelihood:
   low; two independently reported facts would have to fail together.

**Update, 2026-11-14: dev-kit measurements**
(`notes/2026-11-10-devkit-watchdog-probes.md`):
- On the kit, the ROM's watchdog is armed at entry: `0x1C`, firing about
  4.06 s after the image starts [verified, dev kit].
- The kit's ROM runs from a 24 MHz oscillator with a 48 MHz CPU.
- M5's full frame reaches display-on in about 1.2–1.8 s at those clocks
  [inferred from a measured 374 ms pixel loop], well inside 4 s.

So hypothesis 1 (the reset is the watchdog) still fits. Hypothesis 2 (the
watchdog fires before display-on) holds only if the FM-1's cold ROM runs at
least 2–3 times slower than the kit's warm ROM. That is not measured, so
hypotheses 3 and 4 are back in play. `m5-plan-2` must therefore:
- give a sign before the panel (PA2);
- kick the watchdog;
- measure the clock on the FM-1 itself.

The panel path (PA2, SPI1, the ST7789 stream) has never run on any silicon;
the kit images (M4, M6) do not draw [verified: `firmware/m6/main_devkit.c`].

Whatever the cause, the reset booted the application from flash, not the
ROM [verified: V15 screen, identity]. `lunar_m5_status` was lost with it.

## Next

- **On the dev kit first** (pre-authorised):
  - **K1:** read the watchdog and clock registers after a cold ROM entry.
  - **K2:** time M5's own path to display-on over UART, with and without
    watchdog kicks.
  - **K3:** test whether a RAM mailbox written at entry survives a watchdog
    reset and sends the chip back to the ROM instead of the application. If
    it does, a later run can be read back afterwards. Felucca uses such a
    mailbox for UBOOT entry [reported: Felucca `hal/fm1_sys.h`].
- **A next image, `m5-plan-2`:**
  - kick the watchdog at entry, in every wait and on every pixel row;
  - give a first sign that needs no panel (PA2 toggled at 2 Hz);
  - send display-on right after setup;
  - set the oscillator figure measured on the kit;
  - keep the mailbox breadcrumb if K3 passes.
  It touches the watchdog and the mailbox, which the M5 approval excluded,
  so a second FM-1 RAM run needs a new owner yes covering both.
- **M6 on the FM-1 is held.** `main_fm1.c` kicks the watchdog only at entry
  and after the whole blocking frame, on the same unmeasured panel path. It
  is rebuilt (`m6-plan-3`) after K2 and a working M5 successor.
- The first flash install stays at M8 (owner, 2026-11-10).
