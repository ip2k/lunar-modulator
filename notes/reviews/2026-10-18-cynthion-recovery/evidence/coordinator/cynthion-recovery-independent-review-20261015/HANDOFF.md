# Independent Cynthion USB_KEY recovery review

Status: read-only review completed 2026-10-15. No source edits, builds, device access, or USB actions performed. Task source was commit `0266cd267f1abe40c5332384e68331c4ddc361b2`. The integration checkout advanced during review to `a61af54e7bd816b81241612d7628bead954b04ae`; its relevant key waveform and top-level electrical assignments are unchanged, while it adds read-only terminal diagnostics. `uv.lock` was pre-existing unrelated untracked state and was left untouched.

## Findings

The first owner attempt established that the four-state self-test passed and that the terminal state was FAULT with GPIO output enables released. The status word `0x47d8` came from register layout 3 and cannot identify whether the failure was key packet limit, ACK, WAIT_DP, or SOF. The recovery note records that the subsequent passive `lsusb` had no FM-1 ROM/loader/app. Therefore ROM entry, ACK, and SOF completion are not established. See `notes/2026-10-15-cynthion-usb-key-recovery.md` lines 238–255.

The key bit timing itself follows the recorded protocol: `0x16EF`, MSB-first, 50 kHz D+ clock, 8 us low and 12 us high per bit. The implementation drives both lines push-pull throughout KEY (`tools/cynthion_usb_key/gateware.py` lines 221–235). At GAP it disables both output enables (`gateware.py` lines 347–364; defaults are high-Z at lines 146 onward). The r1.4 board has 1 kOhm series paths from N4/P3 to D+/D−, and the FM-1 is reported to retain 15 kOhm pull-downs while receiving the key (`notes/2026-10-15-cynthion-usb-key-recovery.md` lines 127–147; `docs/10-usb-key-dongle.md` lines 94–97). Thus, during the released 160 us gap, the likely line level is SE0 from the target pull-downs, not the high clock idle produced by the intended Pico path's strong pull-ups. That inserts an extended D+ low interval between packets. The protocol source says “both lines released,” but does not document whether the WL82 sampler tolerates that idle level; call this a concrete waveform mismatch and plausible recognition risk, not a proven failure cause. The Pico reference uses open-drain, changes data while clock is low, then samples on the rising edge (`dongle/firmware/usb_key.pio` lines 3–24). Current Cynthion drive changes data at the key-bit transition; it still provides 8 us before the sampled rising edge, so source evidence does not establish a setup-time violation.

ACK handling is the clearest design-contract mismatch. The project protocol requirements say ACK must be both-low for at least 100 us “while the dongle drives nothing,” and the Pico model samples the 160 us gap (`docs/10-usb-key-dongle.md` lines 284–289 and 417–430). Cynthion counts SE0 only in KEY while its GPIO outputs remain enabled; GAP is not sampled for ACK (`tools/cynthion_usb_key/gateware.py` lines 286–364). A target ACK may persist into the next KEY, but the detector then relies on the ROM overpowering whatever push-pull level the FPGA is producing through the 1 kOhm paths. The receiver path passed disconnected loopback; no ROM clamp, voltage margin, contention, or actual ACK was measured (`notes/...` lines 139–147). Do not label ACK detection impossible: the series resistors limit contention and prior third-party push-pull successes are reported, but that is not proof for this board or target.

The target's 15 kOhm pull-downs also mean a simple high-Z SE0 observation in GAP cannot separate absent/unpowered target from an ACK. A sound redesign needs a source-backed bias/sense strategy; simply counting released GAP as ACK is explicitly unsafe. The Pico design used switchable 2.2 kOhm pull-ups, then disabled them after ACK so the ROM's 1.5 kOhm D+ pull-up could be observed (`docs/10-usb-key-dongle.md` lines 94–109, 297–313). The Cynthion implementation has no such switched pull-ups.

Post-ACK behavior is dongle-generated edge mode, not host USB PHY transmission. `WAIT_DP` waits for a valid J state, then SOF emits 4 us D+ low pulses each millisecond; DONE/FAULT leaves the GPIOs high-Z (`gateware.py` lines 327–406). The USB3343 is configured non-driving in OpMode 01 and is used for RXCMD line-state observation, not PHY TX (source note lines 132–137). There is no `SOF_MODE_PC`, PHY TX path, or gateware-controlled D+/D− handoff in this top level. The top only controls VBUS switches; `target_c_vbus_en` is held low (`tools/cynthion_usb_key/top.py` lines 167–219). Cynthion's official analyzer guide describes TARGET-C as the host side and TARGET-A as the device side; normal data traffic is between those target ports, separate from CONTROL. A timed transition to host SOFs therefore requires an external/operator connection change or a separately designed and reviewed switching path. In this attempt the host was intentionally connected only after terminal high-Z, which was FAULT, so the later no-enumeration check does not test host-SOF behavior.

There is a source-documented analog risk in GPIO-generated SOF: if the target's 1.5 kOhm D+ pull-up is active and the FPGA sinks through the board's 1 kOhm series resistor, the ideal resistive divider would leave about 1.32 V on the target-side node (assuming 3.3 V and ideal resistors). Project docs already identify this as a concern; it is an inference, not an observed voltage or threshold result. The USB3343 RXCMD `line_state==SE0` done detector cannot validate that the ROM accepted the pulse train.

## Recommended next action

Use the current diagnostic-only snapshot revision (`a61af54e7bd816b81241612d7628bead954b04ae`, register layout 4) for the next separately owner-authorized controlled attempt, after the required empty-TARGET reconfiguration gate. Read registers 12–18 after terminal high-Z before reconnecting the host. They capture terminal reason/origin, completed packet count, receiver-live/ACK ticks, WAIT_DP ticks, SOF ticks, low-progress ticks, and last key bit/phase. This discriminates: packet-limit means no ACK recognized; WAIT_DP timeout means ACK path fired but no qualifying J/high was observed; SOF timeout means ACK and D+ rise were seen but the edge phase did not complete. DONE plus high-Z followed by no enumeration would put attention on ROM acceptance/host path rather than the earlier key/ACK phases. This is diagnostic evidence only and does not establish analog line levels or ROM semantics.

Before another waveform change, preserve an independent capture of target-side D+/D− over one bounded attempt if the owner's electrical setup safely permits it. At minimum it should resolve the interpacket D+ idle level, whether the target holds both lines low for ACK while GPIOs are driven, and the target-side low voltage/edge timing during SOF. Do not connect a host during keying: a host reset could add long SE0 and host traffic can confound the ROM's SOF measurement (project docs lines 98–109). Do not add a host-handoff mode or alter output drive from this source review alone; first use phase telemetry to learn whether the actual failure is before or after ACK.
