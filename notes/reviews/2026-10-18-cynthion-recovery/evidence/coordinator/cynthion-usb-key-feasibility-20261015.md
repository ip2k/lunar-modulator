# Cynthion USB_KEY feasibility (source review only)

**Verdict:** Cynthion r1.4 is a plausible custom-gateware candidate for replacing the separate USB_KEY dongle and its external series resistors. It is **not ready to configure or use**. The board already places 1 kΩ series resistors between FPGA monitor pins and the target D+/D− path, and those pins are ECP5 Bank 6 pins. The stock gateware exposes them only as inputs. A safe output implementation, rail/PHY isolation, actual PCB revision, protocol waveform, and ROM response have not been validated. No device was connected, configured, or uploaded during this review.

It also offers an LVDS differential input resource and LVCMOS12 chirp-input resources on those same pins. The target USB PHY is separately wired to FPGA ULPI pins and has an active-low reset resource (`rst=R4`, `rst_invert=True`). ## Electrical/protocol inference

The project’s USB_KEY implementation/documentation identifies D+ as clock, D− as data, and uses an open-drain-style low-or-release drive. If the FM-1’s reported approximately 15 kΩ ROM pull-downs are present during entry, release alone does not create a high level. A 3.3 V push-pull high through the Cynthion’s 1 kΩ resistor into 15 kΩ would give an ideal divider estimate of about 3.09 V and 0.21 mA. This is only a first-order estimate: resistor tolerance, FPGA VOH/drive settings, actual ROM bias, line capacitance, and any active PHY loading must be measured or bounded before use.

Two FPGA outputs could logically produce all four D+/D− combinations: low/low (SE0), D+/D− high/low, low/high, and high/high (SE1). This makes push-pull more promising than open-drain if the key waveform requires a defined high on both lines. It does not establish that all four states are part of the required waveform, that they meet USB_KEY timing/voltage thresholds, or that driving an SE1-like state is accepted by this mask ROM. The key is a vendor recovery signaling sequence, not ordinary USB enumeration. The repository’s simulated dongle and source protocol are not physical Cynthion acceptance evidence.

## Gates before any live trial

2. Resolve the full r1.4 schematic path and datasheets for PHY/ESD/switch behavior. Demonstrate the target ULPI PHY cannot contend with custom drive: explicit reset state and power/IO behavior, not just the platform’s default `aux_phy` selection. Confirm no host is attached to either target port during drive. The owner currently confirms both target ports are empty; preserve that precondition and do not connect a host for this experiment.
3. Confirm Bank 6 VCCIO on the actual revision, select legal ECP5 I/O standard and drive strength, review startup/high-impedance behavior and bit-bang sequencing, and verify the 1 kΩ path/current/line levels with a non-device test fixture first.
4. Compare the proposed waveform against the project’s exact USB_KEY source and timing, including idle, both clock phases, both data values, entry duration, and all four line states. Test against a dummy electrical load before a device.
5. Require a separately reviewed, owner-controlled staged trial: passive identity first, one bounded entry attempt, explicit stop on unexpected enumeration/identity, no automatic reset/retry, and independent observation of UBOOT plus ROM acceptance. No changes to connected hardware from this research task are authorized.

## Limits and next step

This is a feasibility assessment, not a safety sign-off or a gateware design. No Cynthion hardware session, GPIO drive, FPGA build, upload, USB_KEY attempt, or FM-1 interaction was performed. ## Follow-up: Bank 6 rail and USB3343 isolation/observation (2026-10-15)

### Bank 6 rail — schematic fact

On the same exact r1.4.0 hardware commit above, `bank6_7.kicad_sch` names the ECP5 Bank 6 supply pins J6/J7 as VCCIO6_1/VCCIO6_2. The wires at sheet coordinates (38.1, 48.26) → (38.1, 46.99) → (38.1, 45.72) connect those pins to the `+3V3` power symbol at (38.1,45.72). Thus the schematic supplies Bank 6 from +3V3. N4/P3 are Bank 6 PL38A/B, so LVCMOS33 matches the documented bank rail. This establishes the released schematic design, not the measured rail on the owner’s particular board/revision.

### USB3343 reset and non-driving mode — datasheet facts

The exact r1.4 schematic identifies U8 as Microchip USB3343-CP. Its `RESETB` is the `PHY.~{RESET}` net, connected to the ECP5 target-PHY reset resource R4; the platform describes this as active-low (`rst_invert=True`). The USB334x datasheet says RESETB low disables its 3.3 V and 1.8 V regulators; USB operation requires RESETB high; ULPI pins tri-state in reset provided VDDIO is present. The datasheet’s termination table note says the HS USB drivers tri-state with RESETB low. It also states the USB weak DP/DM pull-ups are disabled when RESETB is low. Startup behavior is consequential: after RESETB rises and internal supplies stabilize, the chip applies 15 kΩ DP/DM pull-downs while PLL/ULPI startup proceeds. So reset-low is the most conservative hardware isolation state for the PHY drivers; it must not be called verified on a particular live board without confirming VDDIO and reset level.

For an active receiver, Microchip’s termination table defines `OpMode[1:0]=01` as “Tri-State Drivers” and lists all DP/DM pull-up, pull-down, and HS terminations disabled. Its register description calls this non-driving mode. The USB334x still reports line-state changes in ULPI RXCMDs in synchronous mode; the datasheet defines DP/DM single-ended line states, including SE0/J/K/SE1. This gives a plausible source-supported design for sensing the target side through U8 while the FPGA drives via R57/R58: keep the PHY out of reset, configure a valid non-driving OpMode with DpPulldown=0 and DmPulldown=0, disable charger DP/DM pullups, and use the FS receiver/ULPI RXCMD line-state path. The stock startup sequence itself enables 15 kΩ pull-downs before normal ULPI operation, so the proposed key driver must remain high-Z until the non-driving configuration is confirmed; otherwise a startup transient could load the lines. The exact practical RXCMD behavior in this custom setup has not been tested.

The datasheet also notes approximately 2 MΩ DP/DM weak pull-downs in Low Power Mode. That is high impedance relative to the 1 kΩ FPGA series resistors, but it means “no internal pull” should refer to the normal termination network, not zero leakage in every state. Do not enter Low Power Mode for the key unless its impact is separately evaluated.

### Updated gate and limits

The released schematic resolves the Bank 6 rail: +3V3. The USB3343 has documented states for hardware reset isolation and active non-driving receive, but neither alone proves a no-contention implementation: reset-low suppresses operation/ULPI access, while active line-state reporting requires the PHY running and carefully configured. Gateware must enforce reset-low or FPGA high-Z on all startup/fault paths, keep FPGA N4/P3 high-Z during PHY startup, configure non-driving mode and clear every pull-up/down/charger-pullup control before enabling key outputs, then fail back to high-Z/reset on timeout. Validate this sequence on a dummy line fixture and read back PHY controls before any owner-approved device trial. Board marking/revision confirmation, target-port-empty precondition, measured line behavior, waveform correctness, and mask-ROM acceptance remain separate gates.
